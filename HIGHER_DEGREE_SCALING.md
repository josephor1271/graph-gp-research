# Degree-four polynomial and GeneralGRF scaling

**Campaign terminated. The final analysis covers 14 of 36 scheduled fits, forming seven planner-accepted matched pairs:** six at 5K and one at 25K (r0/H). Poly4 was faster than J5 in all seven, both with walk generation charged and with features reused. At 5K its standalone time was 35–47% of J5’s. In the 25K pair it used 45.76% of J5’s standalone time, with raw RMSE 0.606631 versus 0.606334.

Higher degree did not consistently improve synthetic prediction. Poly4 had lower RMSE than historical Poly2 in only one of the seven comparisons. PR17 also used different trace, Lanczos and GeneralGRF walk budgets, so the historical comparison cannot isolate the effect of polynomial degree. **The original advancement criterion remains unevaluated.** There are no measured 50K endpoints, and these results support neither a 50K extrapolation nor an asymptotic scaling claim.

## Matched models and frozen comparison

Poly4 reuses PR20’s normalized Chebyshev filter, with four shape coefficients, signal variance and noise.
Its covariance squares the full filter before selecting rows. The official GeneralGRF has five unconstrained
modulations and noise. J=5 retains lengths 0 through 4. The sampler takes the normalized Laplacian, including
its diagonal and signed off-diagonal weights. It samples uniformly among stored row entries and multiplies
the load by the row count times the weight divided by the survival probability (0.5).
This gives the corresponding matrix power in expectation. The sampled feature covariance is ΦΦᵀ.
For a matched ideal filter, the expectation of this covariance includes the positive semidefinite feature
fluctuation term E[(Φ−EΦ)(Φ−EΦ)ᵀ]. Finite-walk covariance can therefore differ systematically from Poly4.

Both arms have six trainable scalars and the same ideal initial filter. At β₀ = 2 sigmoid(1), the initial
monomial filter is σ[1, −β₀/2, β₀²/8, 0, 0], σ = softplus(1), noise variance 0.69324718.
The two parameterizations have different AdamW regularization geometry. Training uses exactly 100 updates
at lr 0.05, default betas/epsilon and decay 0.01, CPU float64, four Torch threads and four walk workers.
The deterministic covariance uses eight sparse recurrence products per action, O(8 nnz(L) b) for b RHS.
GRF costs depend on walk count, sparse feature support and iterative solver work.

The prescribed PR16 development sets contain two 1K graphs (replicates 10/11), each with two regimes H/M,
250 training nodes and 250 validation nodes. H uses exp(−1.5L), M uses 0.5 exp(−1.5L) + 0.5(L/2)².
They share graph, split, heat draw and noise (variance 0.1). The loader verifies the saved data, graph and
regime hashes, fingerprint, split and training standardization. These previously observed synthetic datasets
provide paired comparisons. Neither target was generated by Poly4.

## Numerical checks

The existing PR20 tiny checks passed. For J5 we compared the official kernel with dense products of the
same saved walk matrices at initialization and at a nonzero four-coefficient setting
(a = −.35, .15, .03, .01; signal SD 1.5; noise .05). Conversion to monomials used NumPy’s shifted Chebyshev
basis. The coefficient discrepancy was zero. Across states the largest covariance, rectangular duplicate-ID
multiple-RHS, transpose, NLL and gradient errors were 1.12e−16, 1.12e−15, 0, 0 and 2.23e−16.
Both covariance PSD gates passed (minimum eigenvalue ≥−1e−10). These checks establish implemented algebra
for the sampled features. They do not require equality between sampled and ideal covariances.

## Interpretation and resource stop

Poly4/J5 ratios of **group mean RMSE** were 1.00097 on H and 1.00243 on M at 5K. The historical comparison
shows higher degree-four standalone cost for both methods. Poly4 improved RMSE over Poly2 on only one of six
5K datasets. Keep Poly2 primary on this evidence. PR20's five-date traffic comparison still supports studying
Poly4 on real graphs. The frozen advancement criterion requires both 25K and 50K group results.

Standalone charges one measured generation plus setup, training and cold mean. Reused-walk omits generation.
Historical PR17 fits and checkpoints were read without retraining. Timers exclude historical/input validation,
cache and checkpoint I/O, guard replays and residual audits. The new training timer includes recording each
gradient norm; PR17 checked finite gradients. Setup includes construction of the degree-four model and AdamW.
The separate guarded fresh update and mean passed at 5K r0/H for both arms (residuals 1.98e−6 Poly4, 8.03e−6 J5).
The guard intercepts selected dense shapes, Cholesky, and polynomial exact-entry/diagonal fallback. It cannot
certify every allocation. Maximum true residual over the 12 completed production fits was 9.24e−6.

At the original stop, of 36 scheduled final fits, 12 completed, one was interrupted, and 23 were unattempted. The interrupted
25K r0/H J5 job generated its official feature cache in 141.18 s, with 25,000 / 129,294 / 340,858 /
656,942 / 901,232 entries at lengths 0–4. The watchdog exited after the two-hour allocation deadline.
Its last two samples were 1,516 and 5,358 wall seconds into the invocation. The 3,842-second gap prevents
attributing that interval to training, establishing continuous RSS coverage, or certifying enforcement at
the precise deadline. Sampled peak for this job was 1,122 MiB. The cache survives; there is no completed
training time, checkpoint, RMSE or usable matched 25K pair. All 50K fits and guards remained unattempted.
We preserved the original order and stopped. Sizes with missing endpoints supply no speed or accuracy estimate.

## Timing-integrity audit and continuation

All 12 original 5K timings are retained. Their one-second watchdog records span 83–235 s per process; the largest recorded interval was 2 s in every case (integer wall seconds). The last sample exceeds the internal worker timer by 3.4–4.5 s, consistent with interpreter startup and shutdown. Actual phases sum to within 0.08–3.29 s of worker wall time when walk generation is counted only in its generating job. The two largest remainders belong to the two guarded jobs. The records contain no concurrent benchmark jobs. Host-wide competing CPU, thermal pressure, and frequency were not recorded continuously, so residual timing variation remains in the comparison.

The 5K runs finished by 17:28 EST on November 18. macOS recorded clamshell sleep at 17:41:51, followed by maintenance sleeps, including 3,844 s beginning at 17:53:26. This is consistent with the 3,842 s gap in the interrupted worker’s watchdog. The 25K/r0 J5 cache had already closed at 17:30:38, approximately 145 s after worker launch. Generation took 141.18 s and the pre-cache samples show no prolonged gap. Its SHA-256 is `daeb6c5d0bde01c3544e5fa224f789b726a218764a0c173fba09612dd80423fd`; five finite float64 25K-square CSR matrices matched their recorded nonzero counts. The frozen code used W4096, J5, seed 796000, four workers and halt probability 0.5. We retain the generation timing and charge it in standalone cost for both regimes.

At 5K the median **training** times across the six fits per arm changed from 4.92 to 93.48 s for Poly and from 11.99 to 194.86 s for General. Median J5 walk generation was 27.59 s versus 2.37 s for J3. Median mean predictions changed from 0.010 to 0.037 s and from 0.035 to 0.240 s respectively. Most extra wall time was spent in the 100 training updates. The new configuration used 128 stochastic trace probes versus 32, a Lanczos cap of 24 versus 16, eight polynomial recurrence products versus four, and broader J5 feature support with four times the walks. Prediction uses no trace probes. The new driver also records each gradient norm. These measurements locate the extra cost by phase; they cannot apportion it among degree, probe count, conditioning, walk budget or system conditions. PR17 timings were historical.

The planner authorized one fresh retry of 25K/r0/H J5, followed by the remaining frozen order. The separate allocations were four hours for 25K and six for 50K, with an unchanged 1,800 s and 12 GiB per job. A caffeinate-protected launcher recorded elapsed wall time, group RSS and sampled CPU and stopped at a sampling interval over 5 s, frozen before the retry. The first attempt and its logs remain intact. The continuation launcher and logs are in `results/higher_degree_scaling/continuation/`.

### Continuation outcome

The 25K/r0/H retry initialized all parameters and optimizer state afresh. Its J5 worker took 1,036.07 s with a 1.24 s maximum sampling interval and peak 1,047 MiB; the paired Poly4 worker took 541.13 s with a 1.72 s maximum interval and peak 944 MiB. Poly4 used 45.76% of J5 standalone time and 52.03% with cached walks. Raw RMSE was 0.606631 versus 0.606334. The standalone advantage change versus PR17 was 0.8255 on this single dataset. The two valid 25K timings exclude the interrupted attempt. Standalone cost includes the validated original generation time once.

The next frozen job was 25K/r0/M Poly4. At 21:20:41 EST macOS recorded a 75 s clamshell sleep despite the idle-sleep assertion. Elapsed samples jumped from 83.821 to 157.547 s. The watchdog terminated the process on wake (SIGTERM). No completed model or RMSE exists for that job. The 25K allocation used approximately 28.9 minutes; the 50K allocation never started. At this review cutoff, 14 of the 36 unique scheduled fits had completed, one was interrupted, and 21 were unattempted. The prior interrupted attempt on the subsequently completed H/J5 job remains separately documented.

The preregistered criterion requires three matched replicates in both regimes at both 25K and 50K. Only one 25K/H pair is available. Its favorable cost and small RMSE difference cannot settle that criterion. The refreshed local figures in `continuation/cost.png` and `continuation/rmse-cost.png` show the six 5K pairs and the single 25K/H pair. Historical markers cover the same completed datasets. All 14 raw RMSEs were recomputed from saved means. All 140 original PR22 files and the 766 earlier historical artifacts passed their post-continuation hash checks. Both existing suites again passed 9/9 with their three known strict-CG warnings each. The only tracked change in this continuation is this report.

### Final disposition

The planner ended the campaign because the scientific value of further synthetic fits did not justify their cost. The tables, figures and conclusions use the 14-fit cutoff recorded in `continuation/outcomes.json`. For execution provenance, a later authorized session completed three additional 25K fits (r0/M Poly4 and J5, and r1/H Poly4) before the user stopped r1/H J5. Thus 17 completed checkpoints exist on disk. The three later completions remain archived outside the planner-accepted comparison; this cutoff follows the review decision and uses no timing or accuracy filter. The interrupted attempts, their watchdog logs and both continuation histories remain preserved.

At closure both existing environments passed all nine tests. The source and artifact manifests from qualification and both continuations verified, including all 766 historical files and the result and checkpoint hashes for the 14 accepted fits. Finalization changed only this report.
