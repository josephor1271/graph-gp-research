# Degree-four polynomial and GeneralGRF scaling

**Poly4 retained a cost advantage in all six completed 5K pairs.** Its standalone cost was 35–47% of J5’s, with relative RMSE differences between −0.10% and +0.61%. Its advantage did not increase in the historical group-median comparison. A wall-time stop leaves the 25K/50K decision unresolved.

## Matched models and frozen comparison

Poly4 reuses PR20’s normalized Chebyshev filter, with four shape coefficients, signal variance and noise.
Its covariance squares the full filter before selecting rows. The official GeneralGRF has five unconstrained
modulations and noise. J=5 retains lengths 0 through 4. The sampler takes the normalized Laplacian, including
its diagonal and signed off-diagonal weights. It samples uniformly among stored row entries and multiplies
the load by the row count times the weight divided by the survival probability (0.5).
This gives the corresponding matrix power in expectation. The sampled feature covariance is ΦΦᵀ.
For a matched ideal filter, the expectation of this covariance includes the positive semidefinite feature
fluctuation term E[(Φ−EΦ)(Φ−EΦ)ᵀ]. Finite-walk covariance can therefore differ systematically from Poly4.

Both arms have six trainable scalars and the same ideal initial filter. At β₀ = 2 sigmoid(1), the initial
monomial filter is σ[1, −β₀/2, β₀²/8, 0, 0], σ = softplus(1), noise variance 0.69324718.
The two parameterizations have different AdamW regularization geometry. Training uses exactly 100 updates
at lr 0.05, default betas/epsilon and decay 0.01, CPU float64, four Torch threads and four walk workers.
The deterministic covariance uses eight sparse recurrence products per action, O(8 nnz(L) b) for b RHS.
GRF costs depend on walk count, sparse feature support and iterative solver work.

The prescribed PR16 development sets contain two 1K graphs (replicates 10/11), each with two regimes H/M,
250 training nodes and 250 validation nodes. H uses exp(−1.5L), M uses 0.5 exp(−1.5L) + 0.5(L/2)².
They share graph, split, heat draw and noise (variance 0.1). The loader verifies the saved data, graph and
regime hashes, fingerprint, split and training standardization. These previously observed synthetic datasets
provide paired comparisons. Neither target was generated by Poly4.

## Numerical checks

The existing PR20 tiny checks passed. For J5 we compared the official kernel with dense products of the
same saved walk matrices at initialization and at a nonzero four-coefficient setting
(a = −.35, .15, .03, .01; signal SD 1.5; noise .05). Conversion to monomials used NumPy’s shifted Chebyshev
basis. The coefficient discrepancy was zero. Across states the largest covariance, rectangular duplicate-ID
multiple-RHS, transpose, NLL and gradient errors were 1.12e−16, 1.12e−15, 0, 0 and 2.23e−16.
Both covariance PSD gates passed (minimum eigenvalue ≥−1e−10). These checks establish implemented algebra
for the sampled features. They do not require equality between sampled and ideal covariances.

## Interpretation and resource stop

Poly4/J5 ratios of **group mean RMSE** were 1.00097 on H and 1.00243 on M at 5K. The historical comparison
shows higher degree-four standalone cost for both methods. Poly4 improved RMSE over Poly2 on only one of six
5K datasets. Keep Poly2 primary on this evidence. PR20's five-date traffic comparison still supports studying
Poly4 on real graphs. The frozen advancement criterion requires both 25K and 50K group results.

Standalone charges one measured generation plus setup, training and cold mean. Reused-walk omits generation.
Historical PR17 fits and checkpoints were read without retraining. Timers exclude historical/input validation,
cache and checkpoint I/O, guard replays and residual audits. The new training timer includes recording each
gradient norm; PR17 checked finite gradients. Setup includes construction of the degree-four model and AdamW.
The separate guarded fresh update and mean passed at 5K r0/H for both arms (residuals 1.98e−6 Poly4, 8.03e−6 J5).
The guard intercepts selected dense shapes, Cholesky, and polynomial exact-entry/diagonal fallback. It cannot
certify every allocation. Maximum true residual over the 12 completed production fits was 9.24e−6.

Of 36 scheduled final fits, 12 completed, one was interrupted, and 23 were unattempted. The interrupted
25K r0/H J5 job generated its official feature cache in 141.18 s, with 25,000 / 129,294 / 340,858 /
656,942 / 901,232 entries at lengths 0–4. The watchdog exited after the two-hour allocation deadline.
Its last two samples were 1,516 and 5,358 wall seconds into the invocation. The 3,842-second gap prevents
attributing that interval to training, establishing continuous RSS coverage, or certifying enforcement at
the precise deadline. Sampled peak for this job was 1,122 MiB. The cache survives; there is no completed
training time, checkpoint, RMSE or usable matched 25K pair. All 50K fits and guards remained unattempted.
We preserved the original order and stopped. Sizes with missing endpoints supply no speed or accuracy estimate.

The local cost-versus-size and RMSE-versus-cost figures show the single completed size with historical points.
No scaling trend, equivalence or statistical significance can be inferred from these six pairs.
## Reproduction and limitations

Base `3ce520d1fd3d8031759518e1372216ff6348935c` includes merged PR21. Clean upstream pins were
`268fff9a3724cdd480dce4b2946068d8211b4f30` (Fast-Graph-GP) and `274b7ee393ee516585552f053e9d4a9c6ad2222f`
(experiments). Apple M4, 16 GB, macOS 26.6.2, four intra-op and ten inter-op threads. The traffic environment

Use the original cached PR16/17 datasets and fitted records. In a fresh output directory, the commands are:

```sh
out=results/higher_degree_scaling
py=results/traffic_env/bin/python
mkdir -p "$out"
"$py" -u experiment_higher_degree_scaling.py develop
# Only if selection.json has a passing budget, invoke each frozen tuple in order:
"$py" -c 'import json; s=json.load(open("results/higher_degree_scaling/selection.json")); assert s["budget"]; print("\n".join(" ".join(map(str,j)) for j in s["schedule"]))' > "$out/schedule"
while read -r n r g w; do "$py" -u experiment_higher_degree_scaling.py "$n" "$r" "$g" "$w" || break; done < "$out/schedule"
.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
"$py" -m unittest discover -s tests -v
"$py" -m py_compile experiment_higher_degree_scaling.py
git diff --check
```

caffeinate. The wrapper enforces the two-hour wall allocation and samples the whole process group's RSS once per
second with a 12 GiB ceiling. Each driver invocation also sets a 1,800 s alarm. RSS can miss peaks and count
shared pages repeatedly. Historical PR17 timings were recorded separately on the same machine.

The development pass took approximately 354 wall seconds, sampled peak 772 MiB. Compact records include
per-case seed/parameters, all losses, numerical trials and gradients. Final checkpoint and result hashes and
